# Decision Log

## How Mike Makes Decisions

**It depends on how much control he has.** When a decision is fully his to make, he's comfortable pulling the trigger — often after gathering input from his team. He consciously checks himself against snap judgments.

When a decision touches something Matt Gunkel cares about, the dynamic shifts: Mike tends to over-analyze and churn, concerned about how Matt will react. This is a known pattern, not a preference.

---

## What Mike Needs Before Deciding

- Pros and cons
- What's he missing — blind spots or unconsidered angles
- Who will be impacted
- Long-term consequences
- Whether there's a deadline shaping the decision

---

## Decision Examples

### CDW Statement of Work — Gemini Enterprise (The Grove) Training Scope
**Type:** Collaborative, within Mike's control
**Decision:** What to include in CDW's training and change management scope for The Grove rollout — content of webinars, sessions, and training for UCR faculty, staff, and students.

Mike had initial thoughts but deliberately brought in Alexandra Chrystal (ITS Communications Manager) because of her organizational change management expertise. He facilitated an internal brainstorming session, consolidated the team's input, and presented the feedback to CDW as a group. The result was an SOW and content plan that reflected UCR's actual needs rather than a vendor default.

---

### Faculty AI Council — AI Hub Proposal
**Type:** High political complexity, limited direct control
**Decision:** How to influence a faculty AI council toward an outcome Mike and Matt had aligned on, without being heavy-handed.

Matt had strong opinions on how the AI hub conversation should go but wasn't on the council — Mike was. Before the meeting, Mike did pre-work with Matt to get aligned, framing the approach in terms of Matt's priorities to get his buy-in. In the council meeting itself, Mike navigated the conversation strategically — steering toward the desired outcome while giving faculty genuine space to voice alternative views. The goal was collaborative, not coercive.

---

## Handling Uncertainty

When Mike has to decide without complete information, he moves forward with explicit caveats — clearly stating what assumptions he's making. He's comfortable framing a decision as a starting point to iterate on rather than a final answer.
